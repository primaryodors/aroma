2. Local backbone sampling investigation

Current Cartesian minimization permits backbone movement. Local template
release softens anchors and template-derived fold restraints around severe
clashes and adjacent residues. This can accommodate bulky substitutions, but
it does not explicitly explore alternative backbone arrangements across score
barriers. Side-chain annealing preserves backbone positions.

Proposed investigation:
- Target bulky substitutions, persistent clashes, and uncertain local segments.
- Explore small coordinated backbone movements with fixed segment endpoints
  and explicit chain closure, avoiding displacement of the rest of the chain.
- Repack nearby side chains and minimize each proposal.
- Preserve chemical geometry, chirality, disulfides, and mandatory contacts;
  apply the existing final review.
- Compare pocket diversity, withheld structural accuracy, acceptance rate,
  and runtime against current local relaxation at equal computation budgets.

Decisions: proposal method, segment size, trigger criteria, and sampling budget.
Recommendation: investigate a bounded optional sampler before finalizing the
conformer-generation design; adopt it based on measured benefit.

3. Conjugate-gradient fallback

REEC uses L-BFGS, falls back to a downhill gradient direction in one condition,
and stops when its line search fails. It has no conjugate-gradient fallback.

Proposed implementation:
- Report the minimization stop reason: convergence, iteration limit, or stall.
- Try a bounded conjugate-gradient phase after a stall while significant
  gradients or validation failures remain.
- Retain the best coordinates and preserve every acceptance limit.
- Compare rescue rate and runtime against an equal budget of existing
  minimization.

Decision: automatic fallback or initially optional?
Recommendation: start behind an option; enable automatically only if benchmark
results demonstrate useful recovery.

4. Distinct receptor conformers

Distinct stochastic starts do not establish distinct final pocket structures.
Several starts can converge to the same arrangement.

Proposed implementation:
- Collect candidates across batches.
- Apply complete PHEW/internal-coordinate processing and final review.
- Cluster final structures using pocket geometry, side-chain torsions, and
  contact patterns after structural superposition.
- Select validated representatives balancing quality and diversity.
- Save separate PDB and cavity files for each conformer.
- Save a manifest with conformer IDs, seeds, settings, provenance, validation,
  and selection metrics.
- Preserve the canonical active PDB.

Decision: fixed count or receptor-specific count?
Recommendation: initially test up to 3-5 distinct conformers, allowing fewer
when diversity is absent. This is an experimental starting point, not an
established count for every receptor.

Use the local backbone investigation to inform candidate generation and
clustering. Reassess ensemble diversity after any broader helix experiments.

5. Individual conformer viewing

The viewer supports structure switching and overlays. Add a manifest-driven
selector in www/receptor.php and conformer lookup through www/pdb.php.

Display conformer ID and basic provenance. Keep the selected structure,
cavity, and docking poses synchronized. An explicit request for an unavailable
conformer should report that condition rather than silently show another model.

Recommendation: build alongside conformer storage, before ensemble docking,
so candidate structures can be inspected during evaluation.

6. Ensemble docking

Give each conformer its own cavity, configuration, output paths, and stable ID.
Retain receptor conformer identity on every ligand pose.

Decisions:
- Equal docking effort per conformer or adaptive allocation?
- Best-conformer ligand score, consensus, or another aggregation method?
- Does repair update one conformer or regenerate the ensemble?

Recommendation: begin with equal budgets and retain per-conformer results.
Compare aggregation methods using ligands excluded from tuning. Include a
single-structure control with equal total docking effort, separating ensemble
benefits from benefits of extra computation.

7. Broader helix investigation

Investigate whether local backbone sampling leaves important pocket movements
unexplored, including coordinated helix shifts, tilts, rotations, and bending.

Proposed investigation:
- Identify cases with persistent pocket limitations after local sampling.
- Explore bounded helix or helix-group proposals, allowing connecting loops
  to accommodate movement with explicit continuity and closure checks.
- Retain trusted structural restraints outside the sampled regions and preserve
  disulfides, chemical geometry, and mandatory contacts.
- Repack side chains, minimize, and apply the complete final review.
- Measure structural accuracy, pocket diversity, held-out docking performance,
  and runtime against local sampling at equal computation budgets.

Decisions: which helices can move, permitted amplitudes, loop accommodation,
and whether experimental state information should constrain proposals.
Recommendation: investigate before physical MD, using evidence from the earlier
sampling and ensemble stages to select cases. Adopt only validated movements
that provide useful results without excessive structural drift.

8. Optional physical molecular dynamics

Use an established simulation engine as a separate refinement stage. Specify
and record protonation, force field, membrane, solvent, ions, restraints, and
ligand treatment. Apply the same final acceptance workflow to its output.

Decision: is the first objective structural refinement, pocket diversity, or
investigation of a specific difficult receptor?
Recommendation: defer implementation until the objective and benchmark are
selected. Keep this stage optional. Lower optimization scores or longer
trajectories alone do not establish better structural accuracy.
