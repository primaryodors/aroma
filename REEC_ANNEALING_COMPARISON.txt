Expanded REEC annealing comparison
Date: 2026-11-08
Status: complete for this native-engine comparison

Question

Does the current targeted side-chain annealing improve candidate acceptance
or final optimization scores across a more varied receptor sample?

Sample

Retained controls: OR1B1 and OR51E1.
Added receptors: OR1G1, OR2AT4, OR2T11, OR2AG1, OR5V1, OR6P1, OR52E8,
and TAAR5, bringing the sample to ten receptors.

Protocol

- Four seeds: 0, 1, 2, 3.
- Three candidates per batch, 3000 minimization iterations per candidate.
- Paired settings: annealing disabled versus 64 rounds at temperature 12.
- Identical starting-coordinate signatures must match within each pair.
- Execution order alternates across seeds and receptors.
- Fixed clash scale 1 and BW binding-site residue targeting.
- Native validation and independent review of accepted serialized models.
- Experimental templates selected by the existing baseline harness.
- No retry escalation or changes to optimization or validation limits.
- Expected total: 240 candidates, 120 matched pairs, 80 batches.

Interpretation

Acceptance means both native validation and independent serialized-geometry
review passed. A rescue is a failed off candidate that passes with annealing;
a loss is the reverse. Report both even when total acceptance is unchanged.

Final scores are REEC optimization scores, not calibrated physical energies.
Compare final scores only when the relevant candidates have identical local
template-release regions. A lower final score does not establish a more
accurate receptor or better docking performance. Count both small changes and
changes exceeding 0.1 percent; the latter is a descriptive reporting threshold,
not a validated scientific effect threshold.

These generation controls omit workflow contact files, docking environments,
PHEW/internal-coordinate publication, and full workflow retries. The earlier
OR1B1 repair and OR51E2 experimental-coordinate holdout remain separate cases
documented in REEC_BASELINE.txt. Canonical receptor models are preserved.

Findings

Annealing improves the pooled best objective for OR2AG1 by 2.6321 percent
and OR5V1 by 1.3102 percent. OR2AG1 improves the best score in three of four
seeds, with a largest improvement of 6.3578 percent. OR5V1 improves two of
three comparable seed batches by more than 0.1 percent.

OR1G1, OR2AT4, OR6P1, and TAAR5 have some individual seed improvements
exceeding 0.1 percent, without improving their pooled best results. OR2AT4
and TAAR5 have worse pooled best scores by 1.2608 and 0.2105 percent. The
other six pooled best scores are unchanged at the report precision.

Total elapsed engine time: 382.81 seconds off versus 588.24 seconds on, a 53.7 percent increase.
This excludes input preparation, summary calculations, and the initial build.

These results support retaining annealing as a useful optional search method.
They do not establish an accuracy advantage or a universal benefit from
enabling it on every attempt. Selective use and final baseline retention are
possible follow-up proposals; no production defaults were changed here.

Subsequent configuration decision

At the user's direction, annealing now defaults to zero rounds in native
REEC and the shared Python adapter used by dohm and fixfail. The existing
REEC --anneal-steps option remains available; --anneal-steps 64 enables it.
No new workflow CLI options were added. The measurements above retain their
explicit off/on settings and describe the configuration before this decision.
